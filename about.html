About

I’m Miranda, an embedded systems and hardware engineer specialising in wearable devices, PCB design, and firmware. I’m drawn to work that sits at the edge of electronics and the physical world: things you can collect data with, put through the ringer, and use over and over again.

Right now I’m at Carv, splitting my time between Innsbruck and London, building instrumented skis that track a skier’s centre of balance and motion in real time. I’m writing FreeRTOS firmware from scratch, selecting IMUs and ADCs, designing PCBs and FPCs, and testing the whole thing on the hill. Before that I was an engineering intern at NewHaptics, where I built an fMRI-compatible refreshable braille display deployed to researchers at Johns Hopkins.

I graduated Summa Cum Laude with a BSE in Computer Engineering from the University of Michigan in 2024, and then immediately decided to cycle 3,000 miles from the Canadian border to Key West, Florida — 77 days solo on the East Coast Greenway. On any given sunny day you’ll find me out on the road, or daydreaming about cycling.

I want to build the tools that allow you to unlock the research, application, or goal you can’t quite reach with off-the-shelf devices. I love sports technology, specifically for female athletes. Building for women in sport is one of the most important and underserved spaces in hardware right now.
