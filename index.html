About

I’m Miranda, an embedded systems and hardware engineer specialising in wearable devices, PCB design, and firmware. I’m drawn to work that sits at the edge of electronics and the physical world: things you can collect data with, put through the ringer, and use over and over again.

Right now I’m at Carv, splitting my time between Innsbruck and London, building instrumented skis that track a skier’s centre of balance and motion in real time. I’m writing FreeRTOS firmware from scratch, selecting IMUs and ADCs, designing PCBs and FPCs, and testing the whole thing on the hill. Before that I was an engineering intern at NewHaptics, where I built an fMRI-compatible refreshable braille display deployed to researchers at Johns Hopkins.

I graduated Summa Cum Laude with a BSE in Computer Engineering from the University of Michigan in 2024, and then immediately decided to cycle 3,000 miles from the Canadian border to Key West, Florida — 77 days solo on the East Coast Greenway. On any given sunny day you’ll find me out on the road, or daydreaming about cycling.

I want to build the tools that allow you to unlock the research, application, or goal you can’t quite reach with off-the-shelf devices. I love sports technology, specifically for female athletes. Building for women in sport is one of the most important and underserved spaces in hardware right now.

Work

Selected projects, 2020–present

  • Powderhound — R&D Motion Tracker
    2025 — Present
    ESP32-S3 FreeRTOS PCB IMU Sports Tech Carv

    A high-frequency boot-mounted motion tracker for Carv’s data science team. Captures motion data 24–50× faster than Carv’s production sensor, with none of the setup overhead of the instrumented ski platform. I designed the PCB in KiCad, wrote FreeRTOS firmware and custom drivers in C/C++ from scratch, and am currently testing prototypes on the hill in Innsbruck. Details pending approval from Carv — happy to answer questions directly.

    Carv →
  • Misty One — Instrumented Ski Platform
    2025 — Present
    FreeRTOS C/C++ PCB FPC IMU BLE Carv

    Smart skis that stream real-time pressure distribution and 6-axis IMU data from embedded sensors in the ski binding to Carv’s mobile app over Bluetooth LE, for sports science R&D. My scope covers the full stack: FreeRTOS firmware and custom drivers in C/C++; IMU, vibrometer, and multi-channel ADC selection and integration; PCB and FPC schematic and layout in KiCad; and prototype assembly and field testing in Innsbruck. I also built a coulomb-counting test device to characterise battery performance and improve state-of-charge estimation.

    Carv →
  • Coulomb Counter — Battery Test Device
    2025
    C/C++ Firmware I²C Battery Carv

    Standalone test device and firmware to measure accumulated charge in and out of Carv’s production battery over time, producing real-world capacity and efficiency data to validate and tune the state-of-charge algorithm. Integrates a coulomb counter IC over I²C with embedded C firmware logging data for offline analysis.

    Carv →
  • fMRI-Compatible Refreshable Braille Display
    2023–2024
    Haptics Accessibility PCB Assembly Python NewHaptics

    Standard refreshable braille displays are incompatible with MRI environments due to metal components. This prototype replaces metal actuation with pneumatic fluidic actuators, enabling braille display use inside a scanner for neuroscience and accessibility research. Built at NewHaptics and deployed to researchers at Johns Hopkins. My work included PCB population, custom fluidic chip manufacturing, MATLAB/Python scripts for research applications, and direct coordination with Johns Hopkins researchers to define requirements and deliver a packaged device. Winner of the 2024 U of M ECE Commercialisation Award.

    NewHaptics →
  • Tactile — Virtual Study Tool for the Blind
    2023–2024
    ATmega2560 PCB Embedded C Haptics EECS 473 Capstone

    A standalone flashcard and note-taking device for visually impaired students, built around NewHaptics’ multi-line refreshable braille display. Lets students load flashcard decks, flip through cards, write notes via an external keyboard, and save files — no computer required. An LCD previews braille content for sighted observers. Top-scoring EECS 473 capstone; submitted fully functional two days ahead of a truncated deadline. My contributions: state machine architecture, custom C driver API for the braille display (porting NewHaptics’ Python API to ATmega2560), PCB design, SMD soldering, and SolidWorks enclosure.

    ATmega2560 · SPI SD card · PS/2 keyboard · UART braille display
  • HASEL Actuators as a Soft Robotic Gripper
    2020–2022
    Soft Robotics HASEL SolidWorks Research ROAHM Lab

    Designed and built soft robotic grippers using hydraulically amplified self-healing electrostatic (HASEL) actuators capable of lifting and releasing objects, in U of M’s ROAHM and EMBiR Labs under Prof. Ram Vasudevan and Prof. Talia Y. Moore. Tested three actuator geometries (curling, twisting Fibonacci spiral, webbed multi-connected) and two gripper configurations; both successfully lifted ping pong balls and paper cups. SolidWorks mounting design and MATLAB analysis of force output and deformation across geometries.

    Supervised by Prof. Vasudevan & Prof. Moore · EMBiR Lab
  • ACL Imaging Rig
    2023
    SolidWorks Form 3 SLA Mechanical Design Research

    Modular fixture for holding and precisely positioning ACL ligament samples during imaging and mechanical testing experiments in the Arruda Lab at U of M. Reconfigurable for different sample sizes and imaging angles. Designed in SolidWorks, fabricated on the Form 3 SLA printer, iterated through formal design reviews with the ME department chair.

    Arruda Lab, University of Michigan
